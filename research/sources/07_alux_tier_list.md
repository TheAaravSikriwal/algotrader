# Source 07 — "Investment strategies tier list" (Alux)

Framing: "This is not financial advice. It's common sense backed up by data."
Monetisation: the Alux app, an "expert collection" by "private equity insider
Aram Tagavi", 30% off annual membership via description link.

Ranked worst to best: F, D, C, B, A, S.

## F tier — Speculation

Described as gambling dressed as investing: charts, brokers, options, margin.
"Fast money almost always leaves even faster."

Worked leverage example given: $1,000 with 20x leverage = $20,000 position. +1%
= +$200 = **+20% on your own capital**; −1% = −20%; **−5% wipes the account out**.
Identified as how **CFDs** (contracts for difference) work — no ownership of the
underlying, just a directional bet.

Evidence cited:

- ESMA forces CFD brokers in Europe to post a warning: **"74% to 89% of retail
  investor accounts lose money trading CFDs."**
- "Research by Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean showed that over **80% of day traders
  lose money** and only a tiny group make profits consistently."

Also lumps in "high fee manual funds" [sic — presumably managed funds].

## D tier — Momentum investing

Defined here as **hype chasing**, not the academic momentum factor: meme stocks,
sector bubbles, house flipping in overheated markets. "It went up yesterday, so
it'll keep going up tomorrow." Driven by FOMO.

Analogy given: a baby that doubles its weight in year one does not weigh 7 tons
by the end of high school — growth curves flatten.

Evidence cited:

- "ATTOM data shows that flipping profits fell to a **17-year low, averaging
  under 25% before expenses**" when the market cooled **in 2025**. After
  renovations, taxes and realtor fees many flippers break even or lose.
- Bubbles named: dot-com early 2000s, meme stocks 2021, "AI darlings today."
  "By the time they buy in, the insiders are already cashing out."

Ranked above F only because "at least here, you own something."

## C tier — Capital preservation

Savings accounts, CDs, "dipping into index funds without scale." Framed as the
tier where you stop destroying capital. "Nobody gets stupid rich here, but for
the first time, you're not losing anything."

Evidence cited: **"In October 2025, the average savings account in the US paid
just 0.4% interest. Meanwhile, inflation was around 2.9% a year."** $10,000
earns $40 while prices rise nearly $300 — nominal balance flat, buying power
down.

Three stated reasons it still has a place: **liquidity** (instant access,
unlike real estate), **safety** (FDIC insured to $250,000), **habit building**
(mental separation of money, reduces emotional decisions).

## B tier — Long-term compounding

"Everyone agrees, from economics professors all the way up to Warren Buffett,
that long-term compounding is the single best investment strategy for 99.9% of
people. Anyone telling you otherwise is just trying to sell you something."

Rule: low-cost index fund, buy the market, hold, do something else with your life.

Evidence cited:

- **"The S&P 500 has returned roughly 10% per year on average since 1926. After
  inflation, that's still about 7% real growth."**
- **"$10,000 becomes $80,000 in 30 years, $150,000 in 40."**
- "The SPIVA report shows the majority of actively managed funds underperform
  their benchmarks over 10 to 15 years."

Stated reason people avoid it: it is boring and slow, with nothing to brag about.

Not ranked S only because scale unlocks private equity, venture capital and tax
strategies unavailable to regular investors.

## A tier — Active investing

"Long-term compounding but with a personal touch." Own the index, then customise:
more diversification than the S&P 500 alone, tilt toward or away from industries.

At the expert end this becomes **value investing** — finding companies
undervalued today. Explicit caution: "Sometimes a company is cheap for a reason.
Poor management, shrinking industry, a weak product. Other times it's overlooked
despite strong fundamentals. The skill lies in knowing the difference."

Concedes: "Most people who try active investing don't outperform simple index
compounding. Morningstar's data shows the majority of active funds underperform
[their] benchmarks over 10 to 15 years."

## S tier — Ownership and capital gains

Runs on scale and access. Private equity (buy whole businesses, restructure,
capture upside); venture capital (pre-IPO slices).

Evidence cited: **"The US saw $4 trillion in capital gains in 2021 alone, more
than all wages combined."** The ultra-rich don't sell — they **borrow against
appreciated assets**, the "buy, borrow, die" strategy.

States plainly: "S tier is out of reach for 99% of people."
