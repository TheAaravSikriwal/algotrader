# Source 10 — Three-step trend-reversal model: BOS, CHoCH, Fair Value Gap

(Supplied as the second half of chunk 10; the first half was a repeat of the SLC
transcript recorded in source 09.)

Claims: "over 8 years of day trading, I've traded this setup hundreds of times."
Charting on TradingView; referral link in the description. No statistical track
record given; evidence is annotated charts plus screen recordings including one
losing trade.

## Stated philosophy (before any rules)

- "A perfect setup is not a trade that is guaranteed to win... Even when
  everything lines up perfectly, these trades can still be losers."
- Do not size up or attach extra mental weight to high-conviction setups. Reports
  a "C setup" that became one of his biggest winners and an "A+ setup" that was a
  full loss.
- "What makes a good trade is when I'm following my actual rules... it has
  nothing to do with the money made or lost on the trade."
- The asymmetry argument: enter near the start of a trend with contained risk and
  exit at trend completion for "**seven times what our contained risk is**",
  which means "we can be wrong a ton of times. We only need to be right a few
  times."
- "Executing an edge that we know is there from data testing" — testing is named
  as the source of the edge, though none is shown.

## Step 1 — Identify the trend

Two required components:

**(a) Contact points.** At least **two** — stated as "clear responses off at
least two levels" and illustrated with three — where price repeatedly fails to
break through the same area. Interpreted as supply outweighing demand (downtrend)
or the reverse.

**(b) Break of structure (BOS).** Definition given: price pushes down to a swing
low, pulls up to a lower high, then **a candle pushes past that prior swing low
and closes beyond it**. That close confirms a lower low.

Requirement stated: **"we at least need two breaks of structure in a trend in
order to confirm"** the lower-low / lower-high sequence.

## Step 2 — Identify that the trend may be ending

Three signals, all required:

**(a) Candle close outside the contact points.** A large candle pushes out of the
area that previously could not be broken.

**(b) Change of character (CHoCH).** Defined by contrast with BOS: a BOS is a new
low/high in the direction of the existing trend; a **CHoCH is a failure to make a
new low (in a downtrend) followed by a push that closes above the trend's contact
level**. "I need to have a confirmation of a change of character."

**(c) Fair value gap (FVG).** The exact definition given:

> A sequence of three candles — 1, 2, 3 — where the **first candle's high wick
> doesn't overlap with the low of the third candle's wick**. The area in between
> is the fair value gap.

The **50% level of the FVG** is marked with a dotted line and named the
**consequent encroachment** line.

Invalidation stated: "If this candle just pushes up and then immediately fills
back down on the next candle, this is not a proper setup and it's not indicating
a true amount of strength."

## Step 3 — Position

- **Entry:** a limit at the **50% midpoint of the fair value gap**.
- **Stop-loss:** below the prior swing point, "sometimes below the fair value gap
  producing candle" — stated preference is to place it **outside critical areas**
  (i.e. beyond a level price repeatedly failed to break), not at a fixed distance.
- **Initial take-profit:** **4R**, described as "a starting point" that moves
  dynamically.

### Risk reduction rule (explicit)

Once in the trade, wait for the **first new break of structure in the new
direction** — a candle close above the first swing high of the initial push. At
that point, **move the stop to entry (break-even)**. "Now we have a risk-free
trade."

### Take-profit refinement (acknowledged as subjective)

Two confluences, looked for together:

1. **Higher-timeframe fair value gap.** Trading the 1/3/5-minute → look at the
   **15-minute** chart for a "high impact" FVG. Target its midpoint.
2. **61.8% Fibonacci retracement.** Fibonacci drawn from the base of the trend to
   its last point, producing 23.6 / 38.2 / 50 / 61.8 / 78.6. The **61.8 "golden
   ratio"** is called particularly important. Validation heuristic offered: check
   whether *prior* price action already responded at those levels before trusting
   them going forward.

Preferred case is when the 61.8 level and the higher-timeframe FVG midpoint land
in the same zone — "I'm looking for harmonies like this."

Results quoted from the two worked examples: "almost four times our risk", and
"6.5 times what we were risking."

**Trailing alternative:** "Sometimes what I'll do is walk my stop loss in,
trailing each individual fair value gap on these big runners."

Explicit disclaimer on the exit: "This is going to be different for every single
trader depending on the market... There's not one simple all-purpose solution...
effectively you're just reverse engineering your success."

## Losing example shown

A trade where he tried to "front-run the breakdown", got an initial response, then
price moved up and triggered the stop. "I took a contained risk, walked away from
the trade, totally accepting that, yeah, I lost a trade, but that doesn't make it
a bad trade."
