# Source 05 — "Gap and Go" day trading (Ross Cameron, Warrior Trading)

Monetisation: paid curriculum ("Warrior Pro"), daily live "morning show",
repeated calls to subscribe and visit warriortrading.com/strategy.

Disclosure given repeatedly: "my results are not typical", "I have over $10
million in gross profit", "most beginner traders do lose money", "trade a
simulator before you put real money on the line."

## The stated goal

"One or maybe two trades a day where you're minimizing your risk while
maximizing your potential for gain. That way even if you're only right 50% of
the time hopefully you can still walk away with a little bit of profit."

## Stock selection — the core of the strategy

Focus on the **leading percentage gainers and gappers each day**. Rationale: if
a stock moves 200%, capturing a sliver (5%, 7%, 3%, 10%) is meaningful; on a
stock that moves 1.5–2.4% a sliver "is a sliver of a sliver" — not enough profit
to justify the risk, producing an inverted profit/loss ratio and poor accuracy
because such stocks are choppy.

"Gapping up means it's opening higher than it closed the previous day." Cause is
almost always breaking news — earnings, partnership, clinical trial.

### The "2020" rule, stated explicitly

- Price generally between **$2 and $20**
- Float typically **less than 20 million shares**
- "20 million share float, 20 dollar price. 2020."

Exception noted: GME had a 76-million share float but "a very unique very
special catalyst."

### Supply/demand imbalance reasoning

Demand drivers: news catalyst; low price (affordable to retail); low market cap.
Supply driver: low float — the worked example (PIK) had a 7.6M share float found
via SEC filings. "The number of shares available is low, the demand is high, and
that can create an explosive opportunity."

Notes the buyers are retail, not institutional: "an army of retail traders out
there that are willing to buy up stocks up 25-30% in hopes that they go up
another 15-20-30%."

## Process

1. Pre-market (sits down 7–8am), review the **top 5 gappers** on a scanner.
2. Check the daily chart for history, all-time high, potential resistance.
3. Consider which is "most obvious" / trending on social media / what the
   community sentiment is.
4. Move to intraday charts — daily, 5-minute, 1-minute, 10-second.

## Entry rule stated

"One of the safest entries is to find the **first pullback**." Specifically: a
5-minute pullback to the **VWAP** and the **9 moving average**, entering when
the first candle makes a new high.

Second pullback gives "a little continuation but it was a bit extended." Third
pullback "getting pretty risky." A topping tail on a candle makes going long
risky.

Typically trades the 1-minute chart using the 5-minute for reference points.

## Risk management stated

"Less is more. It's not a good idea to over trade. It's not a good idea to take
100 trades a day. It's a better idea to try to get one good trade a day."
Example given: 1,000 shares, 20 cents a share = $200. Build consistency for
10–30 days, then trade more actively.

## Result shown

Single case study: PIK, up ~180–200% on a Walt Disney partnership announcement,
7.6M float, prior close ~$1.84, high >$6.00. Author reports +$13,700 on the day.
