# Source 04 — "Advanced Dollar Cost Averaging" (YouTube)

Opens: "one of the most powerful strategies... it could literally change your
life. No BS, no hype." Closes with a free 7-day wealth training funnel and a
comment-keyword call to action.

## Base claim: plain DCA

Buy a fixed dollar amount of an S&P 500 index fund monthly. Your average
purchase price sits between the prices paid, so when the market recovers you
are in profit **before** the index regains its prior peak — "as soon as price
crosses this white line [the moving average], you're in profit again", unlike
those who sell out or who wait for full recovery and end up "back to square one."

Longer horizons approximate the underlying market growth more closely; shown by
lengthening a monthly moving average from 1 to 12 to 100 periods.

Quoted return figures, sourced by asking ChatGPT: VFINX "11.2%" since inception;
VUG "13.2%" since inception. Then takes 3-, 5- and 10-year figures from a
screener, averages them to "16.24", and says "we'll go conservative and we'll
stick with a 13%."

Describes the S&P 500 as "the top 500 performing companies in the world" and
says the market always recovers "because this is human innovation."

## The headline back-test claim

Using ChatGPT-generated data put into Google Sheets, $1,000/month:

- Starting **1 July 2007** (just before the GFC) → ends at **$247,000**
- Starting **February 2009** (timing the bottom perfectly) → ends at **$223,000**

Conclusion drawn: "you would have way more money now even if you got in just
before the crash" — "almost a 25 grand difference."

## The "advanced" overlay

Track distance above the "50-year mean growth of the market":

- More than **10% above** the mean → hold back **25%** of the monthly deposit
  (invest $750 of $1,000), save the remainder.
- More than **20% above** → hold back **50%** (invest $500), save the remainder.
- When the market falls **below 10% under** the mean → resume the full $1,000
  and deploy the accumulated reserve evenly "across our regular deposits over
  the next 6 months."

Framed as "being fearful when others are greedy and greedy when others are
fearful."
