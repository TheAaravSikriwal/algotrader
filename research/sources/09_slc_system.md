## The framework

**S — Structure.** Determine the higher-timeframe market direction for the day so
you only trade with it. Analogy: swimming with the river current rather than
against it.

**L — Level.** Mark only high-probability levels where price has the highest
chance of reacting. "The price can move anywhere it wants, but if it doesn't hit
my levels, I won't look for a trade."

**C — Confirmation.** Wait for additional signs. "Trading is a game of
probability, and no amount of analysis will ever guarantee 100% success. So
instead of trying to be right all the time, I focus on stacking as many factors
as possible in my favor."

Claimed time cost: "I only need around 10 minutes to analyze a setup."

## Step 1 — Structure (exact rules given)

Trade on the 5-minute; read structure on the **4-hour**.

Three phases only: **uptrend, consolidation, downtrend**.

- Uptrend = **higher highs and higher lows** → look for **longs only**.
- Downtrend = **lower highs and lower lows** → look for **shorts only**.
- Consolidation → **take no trades**.

Worked example: NASDAQ futures, 4-hour, lower highs and lower lows → bearish →
only short setups on the lower timeframe.

## Step 2 — Levels (exact rules given)

Drop to the 5-minute (the execution timeframe). Mark **supply and demand zones**.

- **Demand level:** find a sharp, aggressive move *up*. The area immediately
  *before* that move is the demand level. Logic: that aggressive move implies
  strong buying pressure there, so a return has a high probability of another
  buyer reaction.
- **Supply level:** the mirror image — the area immediately before a sharp,
  aggressive move *down*.

Drawing rule stated precisely: **"I take the last candle before the aggressive
move formed and draw a rectangle around it."**

Only mark levels aligned with the higher-timeframe structure (bearish structure →
supply levels only).

### Two valid setups off a level

1. **Untouched level.** Price has never traded back into it. Plan: wait for a
   retrace into the level, then look for confirmation of rejection.
2. **Broken-and-retested level.** If the level was broken to the upside once,
   require price to **break back down through it and retest it from below**, then
   look for confirmation.

Explicit invalidation rule: "This type of break and retest setup is only valid if
a supply level has been broken **once**. If it's been chopped through multiple
times, it's no longer a valid level."

Explicit caution: "Supply levels aren't magic. They don't guarantee a reversal...
This is exactly why we never blindly take trades just because price touches a
supply level."

In the worked example, one setup was skipped because price moved down into the
supply level but "didn't manage to break cleanly below it" — the break-and-retest
precondition failed, so the confirmation step was never reached.

## Step 3 — Confirmation (the one example given)

Says many confirmations exist; demonstrates one, the **stochastic oscillator**
with "custom settings" shown on screen only (settings not stated in the
transcript — the viewer is told to pause and copy them).

The rule, for a short at a supply level:

> As price taps into the supply level, the blue stochastic line needs to **break
> above the upper level and then cross back down**, which shows that price was
> overextended and is now shifting back down.

Counter-example given from the same chart: on an earlier tap of the level the
stochastic did break above the upper band **but never crossed back down** — no
valid confirmation, and skipping it "would have saved you from entering into a
losing trade."

## Trade management (exact)

- **Entry:** on confirmation at the level.
- **Stop-loss:** "slightly above the supply level" (inverse for longs).
- **Take-profit:** fixed at **2R**.

In the demonstrated trade price reached the 2R target.
