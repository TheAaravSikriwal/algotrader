# Source 11 — Catalogue of technical-analysis tools

A rapid-fire list of TA concepts with no performance claims, no backtests, and no
monetisation beyond a like/subscribe ask. It ends by asking viewers which
strategies were missed. Included here because it defines terms the other sources
use loosely, and because several of its rules are precise enough to code.

## Fibonacci retracements

Identify a swing low and swing high; drag the tool **from the swing low to the
swing high**. Wait for a pullback to a level, **"ideally the 0.382 Fibonacci
level because that's the most common level where price tends to reverse from."**
A touch there is a candidate buy entry. Caveat given: price can reverse from other
levels; combine with other confirmation.

(Note the conflict with source 10, which names **0.618** as the most important
level, drawn high-to-low.)

## Breakout patterns

A sudden significant move in one direction, usually after a **consolidation**
period. Patterns named as precursors: **wedges, triangles, rectangles**.

## Reversal patterns

Named: **double tops/bottoms, triple tops/bottoms, head and shoulders, cups and
handles**.

## Elliott wave

Markets move in five waves, then a corrective ABC set in the opposite direction.
Three validity rules given:

1. **Wave 2 cannot be longer than wave 1**, and usually pulls back to the **0.618**
   Fibonacci level.
2. **Wave 3 must be the longest** of waves 1, 3 and 5.
3. **Wave 4 must remain above the peak of wave 1**, and usually pulls back to the
   **0.382** level.

Trade application given: in a 1-2-3 structure, buy the wave-4 pullback to 0.382.

## Fair value gaps

"A candle forms a significant gap due to an imbalance of buying or selling. Find a
candle with a large body, then draw a rectangle at the gap place **between the
previous candle's wick and the next candle's wick**. This level now acts as a
potential magnet where price may revisit."

(Same three-candle construction as source 10, stated more loosely — no 50%
midpoint / consequent encroachment here.)

## Candlestick patterns

- **Engulfing** — strong momentum in the direction of the engulfing candle.
- **Hammer / shooting star** — rejection, shown by the long wick on one side.
- **Doji** — neutrality.

## Heikin Ashi

Replaces the candlestick chart entirely; gives less noise. Green = uptrend, red =
downtrend; larger body = stronger trend. **"It does not display the real market
price."**

## Moon phases

"Moon phase traders believe that moon cycles are correlated with human emotions
and behavior which could have an influence on the market. A new moon means the
market tends to be bullish and a full moon means the market tends to be bearish.
Today it is used mostly as a confirmation tool."

Presented in the same register as every other item on the list, with no
distinguishing caveat.

## Renko

Blocks form on **price change rather than time** — e.g. one block per 1% move,
configurable. Used to filter noise and identify trends. Green = uptrend, red =
downtrend. **"It does not display the real market price."**

## Harmonic patterns

Four-leg shapes labelled **X, A, B, C, D** with Fibonacci constraints on each leg.
Example given for the bullish bat: X→B between **0.382 and 0.5**; A→C between
**0.382 and 0.886**. Others named: **butterfly, bat, crab**, each with its own
values.

## Support and resistance

Horizontal levels where price has bounced before. Below price = support (buy on
approach); above price = resistance (sell on approach).

**Dynamic support/resistance:** the same idea using an indicator such as a moving
average instead of a static line.

## Trend lines

Diagonal levels during a trending market. Upward = bullish, downward = bearish. A
retrace back to the trend line is given as a buy opportunity.

## Gann angles

Lines radiating at multiple angles acting as levels and as a trend-strength gauge
— steep angles = strong trend, shallow = weak. Setup procedure given: enable
**lock price to bar ratio** in settings, mark the swing low and high of a range,
draw a vertical line at the start of the range, use the trend angle tool to
measure **45°**, then place the Gann fan at that 45° angle.

## Momentum indicators (stated: most effective in trending markets)

- **MACD** — upward crossover bullish, downward crossover bearish.
- **Moving averages** — price above = bullish, below = bearish.
- **Parabolic SAR** — dot below price bullish, dot above bearish.
- **SuperTrend** — green bullish, red bearish.

## Oscillators (stated: most effective in choppy or sideways markets)

- **RSI** — oversold region signals possible reversal up; overbought, down.
- **Stochastic** — both lines oversold signals reversal up; both overbought,
  down. Line crossovers also used as signals.

## Divergences

An indicator signalling the opposite of price. Example given: price makes higher
highs while MACD makes lower highs → **bearish divergence** → take a sell. Applies
to MACD, stochastic, RSI.

## Volume indicators

- **Price volume** — volume bar per candle, longer = higher volume.
- **VWAP** — "the ratio of an asset's price to its total volume"; traded like a
  moving average or as dynamic support/resistance.
- **Volume profile** — volume displayed horizontally; high-volume rows treated as
  key levels.

## Supply and demand / order blocks

"If price moves significantly upwards from a level it is considered a **demand
zone**, and if price moves significantly downwards from a level it is considered a
**supply zone**." Treated as key levels for entries. (Matches source 09.)

## Market structure

- **Uptrend** = higher highs and higher lows. **Downtrend** = lower highs and
  lower lows.
- **Break of structure (BOS)** — price breaks the previous peak *during* a trend,
  continuing it.
- **Change of character (CHoCH)** — price breaks the previous structure against
  the trend, "often signaling a reversal." Example: in a sequence of higher highs
  and higher lows, price breaks the previous low to form a lower low.

(Matches source 10's definitions, minus the candle-close confirmation
requirement.)
