# Source 02 — "Make money every single day" (AI-generated, Gemini)

Framing: two paths — "High-Yield Cash (guaranteed but small daily returns) or
Active Trading (potentially high daily returns, but with a massive risk of
losing your capital)."

## Path 1: Active Trading

- **Day trading highly liquid stocks.** Enter and exit within minutes or hours,
  no overnight holds. "High-volume stocks or index ETFs (like SPY or QQQ) to
  extract small, compounding daily profits from minor price fluctuations."
- **Scalping options.** "Often short-term or 0DTE - Zero Days to Expiration
  options", capturing "rapid moves lasting seconds to minutes."
- **Intraday crypto trading.** "24/7 market volatility", liquid pairs BTC/ETH,
  tight timeframes.

Stated guardrails:

1. **"The PDT Rule: In the US, trading stocks/options actively requires
   maintaining a minimum account balance of $25,000 under Pattern Day Trader
   rules."**
2. Strict stop-losses — "a hard, automated rule to cut losses."
3. Paper trade first — "simulated your strategy successfully for months."

Warning given: "Statistically, the vast majority of retail day traders lose
money."

## Path 2: Daily Yield Harvesting — labelled "Zero Risk, Low Passive Return"

- Ultra-short duration cash ETFs (names ICSH, SGOV).
- Money market settlement / brokerage sweep funds (names Vanguard, Fidelity).
  "Paid out monthly but calculated daily."

## The comparison it presents

Simulated $10,000 over 30 days:

- **Daily Yield Strategy:** "a strong 5.0% APY, compounding mathematically each
  day."
- **Successful Active Trading Strategy:** "a disciplined trader averaging a net
  **0.5% profit per day** after fees and losses."

Caveat given: "While the active trading curve looks highly lucrative, remember
that a single unhedged mistake can drop that balance down to $0 instantly."

## Hidden costs section

- Short-term capital gains taxed at ordinary income rates.
- Wash-sale rule: sell at a loss and rebuy within 30 days, loss not claimable.
- Slippage / bid-ask spread: "If a stock's bid is $10.00 and the ask is $10.05,
  you lose $0.05 the moment you buy and sell."

Ends by asking the reader for capital size, hours available to watch charts,
and technical-analysis experience.
